Introduction

What is Islam?

Islam (الإسلام) means "peace" and "submission" to the will of God (Allah). It is the final message in a long line of guidance revealed through prophets including Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and finally Muhammad ﷺ.

A Muslim is someone who peacefully submits their will to God. Islam teaches that God is One, that He is merciful and just, and that every person is accountable for their own intentions and deeds. Muslims follow the Quran and the authentic sayings and example of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ (the sunnah).

At its core, Islam rests on two foundations: the five pillars (acts of worship) and the six pillars of faith (beliefs). Both are explained below.

The Five Pillars

Five Pillars of Islam

The five pillars are the framework of Muslim life — acts of worship that express devotion and bring a person closer to God.

ٱلشَّهَادَةُ

1 · Shahada — Profession of Faith

أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّٰهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ ٱللَّٰهِ

Ašhadu an lā ilāha illā-llāh, wa ašhadu anna Muḥammadan rasūlu-llāh.

"I bear witness that there is no god but God, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of God." Sincerely declaring this with the heart and tongue is how a person enters Islam.

ٱلصَّلَاةُ

2 · Salat — Prayer

ٱلصَّلَاةُ

Aṣ-Ṣalāh

Performing the five daily prayers — Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, and Isha — facing the Kaaba in Mecca. Prayer is a direct connection between the believer and God, offered at dawn, noon, mid-afternoon, sunset, and night.

  • Fajr — before dawn
  • Dhuhr — midday
  • Asr — afternoon
  • Maghrib — after sunset
  • Isha — night
ٱلزَّكَاةُ

3 · Zakat — Alms

ٱلزَّكَاةُ

Az-Zakāh

Giving a fixed portion (2.5% of accumulated wealth) to those in need. Zakat purifies wealth, reminds Muslims of their blessings, and supports the poor, the needy, travellers, and those burdened by debt.

ٱلصَّوْمُ

4 · Sawm — Fasting

صَوْمُ رَمَضَانَ

Ṣawm Ramaḍān

Fasting from dawn to sunset during the month of Ramadan. Muslims abstain from food, drink, and bad habits, developing self-control, gratitude, and empathy for the less fortunate. The fast is broken daily at sunset.

ٱلْحَجُّ

5 · Hajj — Pilgrimage

ٱلْحَجُّ

Al-Ḥajj

The pilgrimage to Mecca performed at least once in a lifetime by those who are physically and financially able. It is a journey of unity, humility, and remembrance, circling the Kaaba as one community before God.

The Six Pillars of Faith

Six Pillars of Faith (Iman)

The beliefs at the heart of Islam — what a Muslim sincerely holds in the heart.

1

Belief in Allah

Belief in God as the One and only deity, the Creator and Sustainer of all that exists.

2

Belief in the Angels

Belief in the angels as honourable beings created from light who carry out God's commands.

3

Belief in the Holy Books

Belief in the scriptures revealed to the prophets, and in the Quran as God's final, preserved revelation.

4

Belief in the Messengers

Belief in all of God's prophets — and that Muhammad ﷺ is the last and final of them.

5

Belief in the Day of Judgement

Belief that every soul will be raised and held accountable for its deeds.

6

Belief in Divine Decree

Belief in Qadhaa' and Qadr — that God's knowledge and decree encompass all, both the good and the difficult.

The Holy Book

The Quran

The Quran (ٱلْقُرْآن) is the holy book of Islam — the literal word of God revealed to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ through the Angel Gabriel (Jibreel) over about 23 years. It is the final and most complete revelation, confirming and completing the messages brought by earlier prophets.

The Quran is organised into 114 chapters (surahs), which are divided into verses (ayah). It was revealed in Arabic, and Muslims worldwide recite it in Arabic, accompanied by memorisation and reflection.

  • Recitation: Muslims recite the Quran in the five daily prayers.
  • Memorisation: Millions of Muslims commit the whole Quran to memory (a hafiz).
  • Reflection: Muslims are encouraged to understand, ponder, and apply its guidance.

ٱقْرَأْ بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ

"Read! In the name of your Lord Who created."

— Quran, Surah Al-Alaq (96:1)
The Messenger

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Muhammad ﷺ (c. 570–632 CE) was born in Mecca and is regarded by Muslims as the final Prophet and Messenger of God. He received the first revelation at the age of 40 in the cave of Hira. His life and character — known as the sunnah — are recorded in collections of sayings and actions called hadith.

Muslims love and honour him as a mercy to all creation, and strive to follow his example of honesty, compassion, patience, and devotion to God. When his name is mentioned, Muslims say "ﷺ" (sallallahu alayhi wa sallam), meaning "peace and blessings of God be upon him."

  • Known before prophethood as "Al-Amin" — the trustworthy.
  • Prophets before him include Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus.
  • He is the "Seal of the Prophets" — the final messenger to humanity.
Everyday Life

Daily Practice

Islam is lived in the rhythms of daily life — remembrance, kindness, and service.

Remembrance of God

Muslims begin the day remembering God, and weave short prayers (dhikr) throughout daily activities — before eating, travelling, sleeping, and entering the home.

Character & Kindness

Good character is central. The Prophet ﷺ said, "The best of you are those with the best character." Honesty, generosity, and caring for neighbours and family are acts of worship.

Charity & Service

Beyond the yearly zakat, Muslims are encouraged to give voluntary charity (sadaqah) — even a smile is counted as charity. Service to others is beloved to God.

Moderation & Purity

Islam teaches balance — caring for body and soul, avoiding harm, and keeping physical and spiritual purity through regular washing (wudu) and prayer.

Glossary

Common Islamic Terms

Allah

The Arabic word for God — the One and only deity.

Sunnah

The way and example of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.

Hadith

A recorded saying or action of the Prophet ﷺ.

Masjid

A mosque — a place of prayer and community.

Imam

A prayer leader or religious community leader.

Qibla

The direction of the Kaaba in Mecca, faced in prayer.

Eid

A festival — Eid al-Fitr (end of Ramadan) and Eid al-Adha (end of Hajj).

Ummah

The global community of Muslims.

Sadaqah

Voluntary charity given for the sake of God.

Bismillah

"In the name of God" — said before beginning any good action.
